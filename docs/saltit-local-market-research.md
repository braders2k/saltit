Place name is **Saltdean** (one word), East Sussex, on the coast east of Brighton. Catchment used here: Saltdean, Rottingdean, Peacehaven, Woodingdean, and wider Brighton & Hove. Domain, DNS, and hosting are out of scope.

### Gaps

- **Evening and weekend on-site from someone based in the Deans.** Cranfield stops at Friday 5pm. Click Resolve publishes hours to 6pm. Diamond is a shop that shuts Saturday at 1pm. LFX has paused home visits. AI Handyman is the main published exception (to 7pm, Sunday by arrangement) and is a Brighton handyman, not a Saltdean IT practice.
- **A named local who will sit with an older person.** Reviews ask for this (Saltdean Tech, Click Resolve). Tuition exists (Southern PC, Lewes Digital) but almost nobody leads with it and a fixed “lesson” price.
- **Smart home beyond the TV.** Streaming sticks and smart-TV Wi-Fi show up on AI Handyman’s list. Doorbells, heating, cameras, and mesh kits are not a local specialist offer.
- **Same-day in Saltdean/Peacehaven, stated honestly.** Claimed by Click Resolve and AI Handyman, and by the KB template. Not offered as a promise by the weekday-appointment pages.
- **Fixed prices in the catchment.** The businesses physically closest to Saltdean (Saltdean Tech, Southern PC, Cranfield) do not publish prices. The clear price lists are Brighton-wide.
- **Woodingdean is barely named.** It appears on Cranfield’s Rottingdean list and sits in BN2, but there is no Woodingdean specialist page with substance.
- **“Saltdean” as a brand.** Saltdean Tech already occupies the place name on Nextdoor. Salt IT still has a gap if it shows published prices, real hours, and reviews under a consistent name.

## 2. Local pain points (ranked)

**High — home Wi-Fi, router, and “the internet is slow.”**  
Which?’s broadband survey (about 4,350 UK customers, Dec 2024–Jan 2025) found 85% had at least one connection problem in the year: frequent slow downloads/uploads 28%, very slow speeds 26%, dropouts 22%, router problems 21%. Every local household page leads with Wi-Fi (Cranfield, Lewes Digital, AI Handyman, Southern PC, SBG). AI Handyman specifically blames thick walls and crowded Wi-Fi in Brighton & Hove housing, and names seafront blocks in Hove. Local outage anecdotes for Saltdean and Peacehaven exist on istheservicedown, but the quotes look recycled and are a **weak** source for “the area is down right now.” Rank is high because of national evidence plus competitor menus, not because of a current local outage count.

**High — printer that has fallen off the network, especially after a new router.**  
Which? (printer survey, March 2024, 9,345 people) lists bad print quality, ink, paper feed, and unreliable Wi-Fi among the reasons people give up on printers. AI Handyman, LFX’s old home-visit page, Southern PC, and a Click Resolve review all treat printers as a standard call. Epson-commissioned UK parent research (2024) put running out of ink and “printer panic” in the top household tech rows, behind Wi-Fi.

**High — slow laptop, new computer setup, email that has stopped.**  
AI Handyman publishes these as its common Brighton software jobs (startup bloat, failed Windows update, email/account config). Click Resolve reviews are full of email, slow PCs, Windows 11 moves, and “don’t make me buy a new one.” Lewes Digital sells new-device setup and data migration as its own service. This is the core paid visit.

**High — helping an older relative, without jargon.**  
Census 2021, Rottingdean Coastal ward (then covering Rottingdean and west Saltdean, among other areas; 14,294 usual residents): **25.7% aged 65 and over**. ONS puts England and Wales at **18.6%** aged 65+ in the same census. Click Resolve reviews name an elderly relative and a parent’s computer, and praise patience. Saltdean Tech’s Nextdoor review is about customers with no IT knowledge. AI Handyman’s home-visit page is written at “older residents” and says they write steps down and will pop back. A 2010 Rottingdean directory listing for home lessons for older people is **stale** and should not be treated as a live competitor. Peacehaven and East Saltdean 2021 age percentages were **not** retrieved in this pass (population counts only: Peacehaven parish area about 15,440; East Saltdean about 3,315; East Saltdean & Telscombe Cliffs about 7,393, via censusdata.uk aggregations). 2011 localstats for East Saltdean & Telscombe Cliffs (median age 44) is too old to lean on.

**Medium — virus, scareware, and “Microsoft/BT called and I let them on.”**  
AI Handyman has a specific warning: fake Microsoft or BT remote-access calls, and tells people not to turn the PC back on. TransUnion/YouGov (reported 2025) found 70% of UK adults had received a scam message appearing to come from a trusted brand. Checkatrade’s UK guide prices virus/malware removal at about £70. Local review volume for viruses is thinner than for “slow” and “email,” so this is medium as a search topic and high as a trust topic.

**Medium — streaming sticks and smart TV.**  
On AI Handyman’s home-visit menu (Netflix, Chromecast, Apple TV, soundbar). Not a theme in Saltdean Tech, Southern PC, or Click Resolve reviews. Real, but secondary to Wi-Fi and the PC.

**Low — full smart-home installs, and pure shop hardware (screens, water damage).**  
Little local advertising for hubs, doorbells, or heating. Screen and liquid damage sit with Diamond, Click Resolve, and the shop trade, and are a different job from household IT support.

**Area factors to use carefully.** The coastal ward that includes Rottingdean and west Saltdean is older than England and Wales, which supports a patient, in-home offer. Peacehaven is described by Lewes Digital as families, home workers, and small businesses; that page is marketing copy. Housing-specific dead zones are documented by a competitor for **Hove**, not for Saltdean bungalows. Do not claim a Saltdean cliff-Wi-Fi problem without a local source.

## 5. Service model

**Households here are sold, and seem to expect, a person at the door.** Southern PC, Cranfield, Lewes Digital, Click Resolve, KB’s page, and AI Handyman all lead with “we come to you.” Saltdean Tech’s Nextdoor line is “callout or remote fix.” AI Handyman’s home-visit page argues remote is the wrong default: people have been burned by fake Microsoft/BT remote access, and a router in a bad spot cannot be fixed over the internet. Click Resolve reviews show both modes in real use — a remote virus clean-up of about an hour, and same-day home visits for an elderly relative.

Remote is the **cheap, second** channel wherever a price exists: 118 “from £10” if the internet works; Brighton I.T. £17.50 per 30 minutes. LFX, having paused visits, now points software jobs at remote and hardware at the Brighton workshop. That leaves a hole for anyone who cannot carry a PC into town.

**Recommendation: both, with on-site as the product.**

- **On-site:** Wi-Fi and router placement, printers, new-device setup with the owner present, anything for an older relative, scam recovery, and any job where remote access itself is the worry.
- **Remote:** slow-PC tidy-up, email and account settings, a short follow-up on a machine you have already seen, when broadband is up and the customer is comfortable.
- **Workshop:** do not offer one on day one. Diamond already owns drop-off Brighton. Collection only if a job cannot be finished in the house.

State hours in public. A credible local gap is **after 5–6pm and Saturday** on-site. Promise only the hours that can actually be kept. Same-day is worth offering as “ask, don’t assume,” which is how AI Handyman phrases it.

## 6. Pricing structure (retainer vs one-off)

**Household demand on the public record is per visit.** No Saltdean, Peacehaven, or Rottingdean page was found selling a monthly home retainer. The only contract-shaped household figure is Checkatrade’s **UK** average annual computer maintenance contract, **about £200** (May 2024 guide, not a local quote).

Small-business retainers are a different market, and they are priced per user: Fixio about **£37 or £59 per person per month** ex VAT (12-month minimum, Brighton among its bases, on-site extra); Black Sheep from **£35 per user per month** (Brighton/Hove, mostly remote, aimed at roughly 10–250 people); Brighton Business IT **£60 per user per month** bundled with Microsoft 365. AI Handyman offers rolling monthly retainers **with no public price**, and says a 2–3 person firm is often better calling **3–5 times a year** than paying a retainer. Lewes Digital will quote an ongoing package and does not publish it.

**Day-one structure for Salt IT**

- **Household: one-off only.** Publish the mid band in section 3 (first hour including travel, a remote session price, and two or three fixed jobs). Take payment per visit. A useful local habit, already in reviews, is a short return on the **same** problem without a second call-out (Click Resolve; AI Handyman’s “pop back if a step confused you”).
- **Do not lead with a monthly household retainer.** There is no public local price to copy, and a small monthly fee with nothing included will look like a bill for silence.
- **Optional later, not day one:** an annual home check near the only public benchmark (**around £200**, labelled as a test against the national guide, not as “the local rate”). Include one visit plus a remote follow-up, or don’t sell it.
- **Light small business:** first visit on the same one-off rates. A monthly block of hours only if they ask after that visit. Do not publish £35–£60 per user — that is managed IT for larger teams, and it is the wrong shop window for this catchment.
